Unlike most of the games and prototypes here, which began around 2026, Amoeba Rogulike is from all the way back in 2021. It was my first game jam entry! The [7drl](https://itch.io/jam/7drl-challenge-2021). It was [ranked 1st](https://itch.io/jam/7drl-challenge-2021/results) based on aggregate metric score, though it was shared with 5 other entries. Still, out of 240 total submissions that year, I was extremely happy with the result.

<!--more-->

I still really like the idea of the game. I think it's really unique! I had this idea in my head for a long time of a game where you would go around as a cool goo monster and manage internal "organells" kind of like a tower defense / factory game. A traditional Roguelike probably wasn't the best way to represent that at the time, though. But it's what I was technically capable of! So it's what I went with.

This definitely could have been a full game if I had put my time and focus into it. I didn't really want to be a game developer, though. In hindsight, I probably could have put some time into it, and it would have been awful ROI. I had a lot going for me, admittedly! I had strong social connections in the Roguelike community which I think is the most important thing you could have as a game developer. Unfortunately, that wouldn't have been able to stop the rising tide of AI that would make all my efforts in game programming moot. So I'm quite lucky I didn't invest in it!

If I had to list my main issues with it, it would just be that the design itself is rather bad. It's a hodgepodge of mechanics added because I thought they were "cool", not because I was looking at them through the lens of a designer. More attention to detail without adding anything *new* would have gone a long way, I think.
- Better organelle-tree feedback
- Randomly putting random junk on the ground???
- Information overload
- Tactical decisions don't really matter, the winning strategies for the game relate to power-scaling in a corner.
- Game overstays its welcome by a lot
- Early game too slow
- Pacing is nonsensical, you're either dramatically under-power-scaled or dramatically over-power-scaled.
- Tighten tighten tighten! Smaller numbers! I love small numbers and hate big numbers!

When I remastered the game earlier this year to put it on my website with all the other stuff, I did a couple of low hanging fruit improvements (bug fixes, tiny balance tweaks, and visuals/sounds/animations for telegraphing). I think those were worthwhile. I regret making the map easier on the lower difficulty, though. It made the game worse and kind of defeated the purpose of making it a "remaster". I'll back out that change when I get around to it.

Doing a game jam was weird. I think it was a valuable learning experience and I don't regret it. However, I did this game jam while I was still in school, mid-pandemic. I didn't have a job or real adult life, yet. I do not think I would do something like that again unless I become both unemployed and independently wealthy with something to prove. Obviously, these days, coding skills are worth next to nothing, so I don't even think it would be worth my time then, but who knows.

The [original submission is still on itch](https://vectis.itch.io/amoeba-roguelike) if you want it for some reason, though I personally think it's abhorrently inaccessible. Plus, I kind of have beef with itch.io right now because of the crazy amount of censorship + takedowns that they do, and I kind of want to pull it ideologically... But alas, I want this piece of early adulthood fossilized in amber more than I want to protest something by moving the host for a game nobody plays anyway. I'm not *that* petty.
