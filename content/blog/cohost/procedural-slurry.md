Since [AI Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Dungeon)'s heyday, I've wondered about a better way to generate content™. Usually, pondering this, I assume that the purpose of generated content in this domain is to adhere to a couple sets of strict standards a conventional author might like. But after reading this, I'm not so sure.

<!--more-->

## The Idea
I focus solely in the realm of text adventures ([Interactive Fiction](https://ifdb.org/), for pedants) for the sake of simplicity, since even in this nascent form it gives me the core parameters I've been grappling with:
* The content is narrative in nature
* The minimum viable product is cheap to produce
* A large, mature body of existing media to use as reference material
* I bought [50 Years of Text Games](https://if50.textories.com/)

Okay with that out of the way... So you know that thing about AI and GPT and all that? It's cool, it generates legible text and can be applied a little creatively for a system of classifiers- in turn leading to a slew of manners of "gamification". But let's throw that all out the window and focus on something else instead:

Let's say we abducted some authors from all over the world. Some of them speak English well, some barely speak it at all, doesn't matter- they're locked in a basement. More practically, we would hire people to do the work described, but the locked-in-a-basement thing is a useful way of looking at it because you don't have to think about costs, ethics, etcetera. They sit down at their computers, white text on a black background, and then... Boom. Chained to their chairs, launched right into the [Twine](https://twinery.org/) user interface. They get a typical text adventure product, sure but something's different!
